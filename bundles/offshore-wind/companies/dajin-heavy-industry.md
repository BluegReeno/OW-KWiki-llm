# Overview

Dajin Heavy Industry is a Chinese manufacturer of heavy steel structures for
the wind industry, including offshore wind foundation components. It sits in
the same [fixed-bottom foundation](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md)
supply-chain segment as European and Korean fabricators such as
[EEW Group](/companies/eew-group.md) (monopiles) and
[CS Wind Offshore](/companies/cs-wind-offshore.md) (transition pieces).

# Key projects

- **[Gennaker](/projects/gennaker.md) (Germany, Baltic Sea)**: fabricating
  the 63 transition pieces for [Skyborn Renewables](/companies/skyborn.md)'s
  976.5 MW wind farm; fabrication started in August 2026. This is a Chinese
  supplier feeding a large German offshore project, a route into Europe also
  taken from Asia by Korean fabricators.

- **[Hornsea 3](/projects/hornsea-3.md) (UK, North Sea)**: supplying XXL
  monopiles for the 2.9 GW [Ørsted](/companies/orsted.md) project alongside the
  Spanish fabricator [Haizea Wind Group](/companies/haizea-wind-group.md), with
  the monopiles shipped from China to the UK on Dajin's own deck carrier.

- **[Inch Cape](/projects/inch-cape.md) and Moray West (UK, Scotland)**:
  named as further European projects for which Dajin has supplied foundations,
  Moray West having taken a final batch of XXL monopiles from the company.

Contract value, contract date, fabrication yard, delivery schedule and
shipping arrangements were not reported in the source item.

# European footprint

Dajin's route into Europe has so far been fabrication in China plus shipping on
its own vessels. In **August 2026** it signed a Memorandum of Understanding
with [Haventus](/companies/haventus.md), owner and developer of **Ardersier
Port** in Scotland, to explore combining its manufacturing and transport
capability with the port's infrastructure in support of both
[fixed-bottom](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md) and
[floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) projects across the UK and
Europe. The parties will also assess how Ardersier could serve Dajin as a site
for storage, marshalling, assembly and transportation of components — bringing
its logistics capability closer to European projects, in the words of business
development director Artur Pielech. No investment, lease or volume commitment
was reported.

# Vessels

Dajin also operates its own **King-class** offshore wind transport vessels,
a self-developed series, making it both a fabricator and a shipowner moving
its own foundation components.

- **King Two** — delivered on 5 August 2026, the second vessel of the series.

Vessel dimensions, deck area and cargo capacity, builder and yard, first
assignment and the delivery date of the first King-class vessel were not
reported in the source excerpt.
