# Mitsui O.S.K. Lines (MOL)

Mitsui O.S.K. Lines is one of Japan's largest shipping groups. Its
offshore wind activity runs through subsidiaries **MOL Drybulk** (vessel
ordering and commercial support) and **MOL Coastal Shipping** (domestic
coastal operations), which together are building out logistics capacity
for Japan's offshore wind build-out.

## Wind Whale - Japan's first coastal deck carrier for offshore wind

MOL, MOL Drybulk and MOL Coastal Shipping held a naming and completion
ceremony for the deck carrier **Wind Whale** on 29 July 2026 in Nagasaki.
It is described as Japan's first coastal deck carrier purpose-built to
transport offshore wind turbine foundation components.

* Built by China's Taizhou Sanfu Ship Engineering, under a contract MOL
  Drybulk signed with the yard in 2024.
* 12,709 dwt, 149.9 metres long.
* Flush deck allowing large components — monopiles, towers, blades,
  nacelles and floating foundations — to be rolled on directly from the
  stern or sides using multi-axle transporters.
* Dynamic positioning (DP) system, enabling direct cargo transfer to
  wind turbine installation vessels (WTIVs) offshore, which MOL presents
  as more flexible than non-self-propelled barges.
* Jointly owned by MOL and MOL Drybulk, operated by MOL Coastal Shipping
  with support from MOL Drybulk.

Its stated role is to carry
[monopile foundations](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md)
manufactured at JFE Engineering's Kasaoka facility in Okayama Prefecture
to offshore wind construction sites around Japan — domestic component
transport for projects such as
[Oga-Katagami-Akita](/projects/oga-katagami-akita.md).

## Ta San Shang Marine (TSSM) - CSOV joint venture

MOL is a partner in **[Ta San Shang Marine](/companies/ta-san-shang-marine.md)**
(TSSM), a joint venture that on 10 August 2026 launched a new
commissioning service operation vessel (CSOV) in Vietnam. The reporting
excerpt is cut off before the other joint venture partner is named, and
gives no vessel name, design, builder, delivery date or charter — the
offshoreWIND.biz article is filed under Damen and Ha Long Shipyard. It
places MOL in offshore wind service tonnage alongside its domestic
Japanese component-logistics fleet.
