# Overview

The Port of Darłowo is a Polish port on the central Baltic coast, being
adapted to serve as an operations and maintenance service base for offshore
wind farms in the Polish Baltic Sea. Two parallel infrastructure programmes,
with a combined value of more than PLN 174 million (EUR 40.3 million), were
reported complete on 20 August 2026. Roughly PLN 144 million (EUR 33.3
million) of the total came from Poland's National Recovery and Resilience
Plan (KPO).

# Breakwater and navigation safety project

Led by the Maritime Office in Szczecin and valued at approximately PLN 130
million (EUR 30.1 million) — including around PLN 109 million (EUR 25.2
million) of KPO funding — this project reconstructed the port's 356.5-metre
western breakwater and added a new 100-metre spur to reduce wave activity in
the outer harbour, together with new lighting and a navigation beacon. The
rebuilt breakwater is designed for greater resistance to erosion.

Construction started after the Maritime Office signed a contract with
contractor DORACO in February 2025 and finished in July 2026. It consumed
around 980 tonnes of reinforcing steel, 8,000 m³ of hydraulic concrete,
nearly 16,000 m² of sheet piling and 189,000 tonnes of rock. The upgraded
approach is intended to give safer access to commercial and tourist vessels
as well as to offshore wind service vessels.

# Offshore wind service base project

The second project, led by the City of Darłowo and valued at more than PLN
44 million (EUR 10.2 million) — including PLN 35 million (EUR 8.1 million)
in KPO funding — is aimed specifically at preparing the port as an offshore
wind service base. It modernised nearly 400 metres of the existing Southern,
Gdynia and Szczecin quays and connected them functionally to a new
229-metre Transhipment Quay sized for specialised offshore wind service
vessels.

The facilities are positioned to serve Polish Baltic offshore wind farms
such as [Baltic Power](/projects/baltic-power.md) and
[Bałtyk 2 & 3](/projects/baltyk-2-3.md).
