# Denmark's 2.8 GW CfD Offshore Wind Tender (2025-2026)

Denmark's current offshore wind tender covers three sites totalling 2.8 GW of
minimum capacity and is run by the Danish Energy Agency. It was launched in
November 2025 under a redesigned support scheme, after the country's previous
3 GW tender — offered without state support — attracted **zero bids** in late
2024.

## Redesign after the 2024 zero-bid failure

In response to the failed round, the Danish government replaced the
unsupported model with **two-sided Contracts for Difference** backed by state
aid, and extended project timelines to improve investment conditions. The
lowest strike price wins; the awarded price is guaranteed for **20 years** from
commissioning of each wind farm. Consents run for **30 years**, extendable by a
further 10.

## Sites and results

| Site | Minimum capacity | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [North Sea I Mid (Nordsøen Midt)](/projects/nordsoen-midt.md) | 1 GW | Awarded to Vattenfall, August 2026 |
| [Hesselø](/projects/hesselo.md) | 800 MW | Awarded to Vattenfall, August 2026 |
| Nordsøen Syd (North Sea South) | 1 GW | Still open, bids due 20 October 2028 |

Bids for the first two sites closed on 20 May 2026. The Danish Energy Agency
received **two bids for North Sea I Mid and five for Hesselø**, and on 4 August
2026 named [Vattenfall](/companies/vattenfall.md) the winning bidder for both:

* **North Sea I Mid** — CfD strike price **DKK 504/MWh (~EUR 67.60)**, awarded
  to Vattenfall Vindkraft A/S on behalf of a company yet to be established.
* **Hesselø** — CfD strike price **DKK 542/MWh (~EUR 72.70)**, awarded to
  Vattenfall Vindkraft Offshore Wind DK P/S.

Both projects are to be fully operational by 2032, for a combined minimum of
1.8 GW — enough, per the Danish Energy Agency, for the annual consumption of at
least 1.8 million Danish and European households. Grid connection has been
reserved for both, with the connection point around 50 km inland.

Contract signature is subject to completion of formal verification procedures,
including documentation checks and compliance with procurement requirements.

These are the first contracts awarded under the revamped Danish framework, and
they land in the same 2026 wave of European moves back to two-sided CfDs as
[Belgium's Princess Elisabeth Zone Site 1 tender](/tenders/belgium-princess-elisabeth-zone-pez-1.md).
