# Overview

Offshore energy storage covers systems that absorb surplus electricity from
offshore wind farms (and other renewables) at sea, releasing it when demand or
prices rise. The motivation is the same as for onshore storage — smoothing an
intermittent output and shifting it in time — but siting the storage offshore
lets it exploit properties of the marine environment that are unavailable on
land, chiefly water depth and hydrostatic pressure.

The field is at demonstrator stage in 2026. It sits alongside
[Power-to-X](power-to-x.md) as the second family of approaches to decoupling
offshore generation from instantaneous
[grid export](hvdc-grid-connection.md): Power-to-X converts the electricity into
another carrier (usually hydrogen), while storage keeps it as electricity to be
returned to the grid later.

# Marine Pumped Hydro

The most-developed concept borrows the principle of pumped hydro, which onshore
depends on an elevation difference between two reservoirs. Offshore, that
elevation difference is replaced by the pressure difference between the sea
surface and the seabed: energy is stored by pumping water (or a denser fluid)
against hydrostatic pressure, and recovered by letting it flow back through
turbines.

Because no mountain topography is required, the approach can in principle be
co-located with a wind farm rather than sited wherever the terrain allows.

## Sizable Energy demonstrator (Calabria, Italy)

The Italian startup **Sizable Energy** began testing a pre-industrial-scale
demonstrator in 2026 at the **Natural Ocean Engineering Laboratory (NOEL)**, the
University Mediterranea of Reggio Calabria's offshore test site off the Calabrian
coast. Installed in April 2026, the demonstrator is a **floating reservoir 50
metres in diameter**.

Its cycle works as follows:

- **Charging**: surplus renewable electricity pumps a **dense brine solution**
  into the floating reservoir.
- **Discharging**: when demand rises, the brine is released to a **second
  reservoir on the seabed**, driving turbines that generate power.

Sizable Energy positions the technology as an alternative to battery energy
storage systems (BESS). The NOEL campaign is intended to validate the concept at
pre-industrial scale and to refine launch and installation methods ahead of
commercial deployment.

The installation itself illustrates the marine-logistics dimension of the
technology: France-headquartered logistics provider **GEODIS** said on 29 July
2026 that it had carried out the engineering for launching, towing and
installing the tank — lifting, transport and installation engineering including
crane positioning, lifting plans, towing procedures and mooring operations, plus
on-site technical supervision during deployment. The reservoir weighs
approximately **11 tonnes** and required a **350-tonne crane with roughly 50
metres of outreach** to launch within the site's constraints.

# Co-Location Under Shared Offshore Transmission

A contrasting route keeps the battery onshore but attaches it to the wind farm's
own [grid connection](hvdc-grid-connection.md) instead of giving it a separate
one. The UK's **Offshore Transmission Network Review (OTNR)** enables such
large-scale co-location, and the first utility-scale example is **Iceni**, a
300 MW / 600 MWh Tesla battery sharing the transmission infrastructure and
onshore connection point of [Hornsea 3](/projects/hornsea-3.md) near Norwich.
[Ørsted](/companies/orsted.md) took FID on it in June 2024, broke ground in
March 2025, and in August 2026 selected [Arenko](/companies/arenko.md)'s Nimbus
platform to optimise and trade the asset; operations are scheduled for Q1 2027.
[Arenko](/companies/arenko.md) calls it the world's first utility-scale battery
directly integrated into offshore transmission infrastructure.

The engineering interest here is not the storage medium — a conventional
lithium-ion BESS — but the shared connection point: two assets with very
different output profiles must be dispatched through one finite grid capacity,
which shifts the difficulty into forecasting, real-time capacity allocation and
controls integration rather than the hardware.

# Other Approaches

Compressed air energy storage linked to offshore wind is also being pursued —
EnergyPathways launched such a project in April 2026 — as are conventional
battery systems installed on offshore platforms or vessels. None has yet reached
commercial scale in an offshore wind context.
