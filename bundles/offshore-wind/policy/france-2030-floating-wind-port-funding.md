# Overview

On 30 July 2026 the French government announced the selection of five ports to
share nearly **EUR 260 million** in funding under the **France 2030** investment
programme, to upgrade port infrastructure supporting the country's floating
offshore wind industry. The funding is administered by **ADEME**, the French
agency for ecological transition, and is expected to leverage almost
**EUR 1 billion** in total investment.

The selection is the industrial-infrastructure counterpart to France's floating
wind build-out under the
[Programmation Pluriannuelle de l'Énergie](/policy/french-energy-policy-ppe.md):
it lands as the AO10 tender is set to award a little over 10 GW across seven
[floating](/technology/floating-wind.md) and four fixed-bottom offshore wind
farms, and after the European Commission cleared France's EUR 63 billion CfD
scheme on 14 July 2026.

# Key Facts

- **Announcement date**: 30 July 2026, following a call for projects launched in 2024.
- **Amount**: EUR 258.5 million combined, under the France 2030 investment programme.
- **Administering body**: ADEME.
- **Leverage**: the port funding is expected to trigger almost EUR 1 billion of total investment.
- **Selected ports**: Cherbourg, Brest, Nantes-Saint Nazaire, Port-la-Nouvelle, Marseille-Fos.
- **Purpose**: adapt port infrastructure for the manufacture, assembly and deployment of floating offshore wind projects.
- **Stated objective**: support France's target of deploying **6 GW of floating wind by 2040**.
- **Industrial logic**: the selected projects are intended to form a network of complementary industrial hubs on the Atlantic, Channel and Mediterranean coasts, strengthening the domestic supply chain.
- **Cherbourg case**: Ports de Normandie said its Port of Cherbourg upgrade represents a EUR 30 million investment — EUR 10.5 million from the ADEME funding, EUR 18.1 million from revenue generated by offshore renewable energy activities, and EUR 1.4 million from the Cotentin Urban Community.

# Award Breakdown and Project Scopes

Detail published on 21 August 2026 set out the per-port allocations and the works
they fund. Across all five, the investments primarily involve the construction or
modification of **quays, storage areas and access infrastructure** sized for the
large components of [floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) — the
manufacture and assembly of floating foundations, and the integration of turbines
onto the floaters.

| Port | Project | Award |
|---|---|---|
| Marseille-Fos | DEOS | EUR 82.3 million |
| Nantes-Saint-Nazaire | Éole | nearly EUR 60 million |
| Brest | InFloW | more than EUR 58 million |
| Port-la-Nouvelle | NOUVEOL | EUR 48 million |
| Cherbourg | Flamands 0 quay | EUR 10.5 million |

- **Marseille-Fos — DEOS**: the largest award, for construction, launching and
  storage of floating foundations across 30 hectares of port land, with 400 m of
  quay dredged to 17 m and a further 31-hectare floating storage area. Total
  investment estimated at EUR 303 million. The port has launched a call for
  projects to select its first industrial occupant; the infrastructure
  design-and-build tender is planned for autumn 2026, an environmental
  authorisation application towards the end of 2026, construction from early 2028
  and phased deliveries between late 2029 and mid-2030.
- **Nantes-Saint-Nazaire — Éole**: centred on a 530 m quay for assembling
  turbines onto floating platforms, sized for roughly 500-600 MW per year of
  project throughput. Total project cost over EUR 200 million.
- **Brest — InFloW**: reinforcement and modification of quays, dedicated logistics
  areas and additional industrial capacity, bringing foundation manufacture,
  assembly and launch together at BrestPort's marine renewable energy terminal.
- **Port-la-Nouvelle — NOUVEOL**: a fully integrated industrial platform for the
  assembly and heavy maintenance of French Mediterranean floating wind farms by
  2030, adapted to the next generation of turbines.
- **Cherbourg — Flamands 0**: a new heavy-duty quay of 140-160 m rated for 15-50
  tonnes per square metre, extending the existing Quai des Flamands, plus moving
  part of the harbour breakwater, extending and strengthening the North Quay and
  dredging. Ports de Normandie says the extra heavy-duty quay lets the port serve
  two projects at once, such as installation hubs or foundation storage.

# Notes

The works schedules for Nantes-Saint-Nazaire, Brest and Port-la-Nouvelle, and the
selection criteria of the 2024 call for projects, have not been reported. The page
is tagged `tenders` because the funding was allocated through a competitive call
for projects and directly serves the AO10 tender pipeline — and because Marseille-Fos
is itself running two downstream competitions: a call for projects for DEOS's first
industrial occupant, and a design-and-build tender for the infrastructure in
autumn 2026.

Port-la-Nouvelle and Marseille-Fos serve the Mediterranean floating pipeline
that already includes [EFGL](/projects/efgl-golfe-du-lion.md), and the funding
sits alongside private-sector localisation moves such as the
[BW Ideol / NGE floating foundation manufacturing facility](/digests/2026-07-23-bw-ideol-nge-floating-foundation-facility.md)
in southern France.
