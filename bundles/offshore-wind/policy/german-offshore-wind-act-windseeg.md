# Overview

The **Wind Energy at Sea Act** (*Windenergie-auf-See-Gesetz*, WindSeeG) is the German
law governing offshore wind site designation, grid connection allocation and the
tender system through which sea areas are awarded to developers. It is administered
by the **Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy (BMWE)**, with site
planning and surveys carried out by the **Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency
(BSH)**.

Germany's statutory target is **at least 70 GW of offshore wind capacity by 2045**.

# Why the Act Is Being Reformed

The German auction design — uncontracted, negative-bidding rounds with no price
hedging — ran into difficulty in 2025:

- In **August 2025**, a 2.5 GW tender for the **N-10.1 and N-10.2** sites
  **attracted no bids at all**.
- The government subsequently **postponed the re-tender of those two sites to 2027**.
- In **June 2026**, the BSH proposed revisions to the design of several German
  offshore wind areas, including N-10.1 and N-10.2, ahead of that 2027 round.
- Industry bodies warned in January 2026 that the failed tender and grid connection
  delays were putting investment and the 2030 expansion target at risk.

# The August 2026 Draft Amendment

BMWE presented a draft amendment on **10 August 2026** and opened a consultation on
it. As reported, the draft would:

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Support mechanism | Introduce **bilateral Contracts for Difference (CfDs)** |
| Auction volume | **Annual tenders of 2 GW to 4.8 GW** |
| Operating period | Longer standard operating period — **35 years** for new wind farms |
| Target | Retains **at least 70 GW by 2045** |
| Scope | Establishes opportunities for **cross-border offshore wind projects** |

# Industry Response (BWO)

The **German Offshore Wind Energy Association (BWO)** welcomed the inclusion of
bilateral CfDs, but objects to how they are framed: the mechanism should be
**part of the tender design itself, not a "fallback option"** activated only if a
site fails to attract a developer willing to proceed without price hedging.

BWO and other industry organisations had already called for CfDs, together with
long-term **PPAs** and the 35-year operating period, in a November 2025 reform
call — the operating period and CfDs now appear in the draft.

BWO's main criticism is an omission: the draft contains **no mechanism to return
and re-tender sites awarded in the 2023-2025 rounds**. BWO proposed such a
mechanism in May 2026 for projects that have not reached FID, arguing that
uneconomic projects otherwise tie up grid connection capacity and sea areas for
years. Under its proposal, developers could hand back sites voluntarily under
defined conditions — forfeiting payments already made, being barred from bidding
again for the same areas, and making preliminary project studies available for
future development.

# BSH Site Pre-Investigation and Survey Procurement

Under the WindSeeG, sea areas are surveyed by the state before they are tendered to
developers: the BSH carries out **site pre-investigation**, and the resulting
geophysical, geotechnical and environmental data are made available to bidders and
to the eventual site holder.

- **26 August 2026** — the BSH **opened a tender for preliminary geophysical
  surveys**. This is a services procurement, not an award of sea area, but it is the
  upstream step that determines when sites can be offered to developers. The reported
  item does not state the areas covered, the contract value, the lot structure or the
  bid deadline.

# Significance

Germany would be following **Denmark**, where a switch to CfDs brought bidders back
to a previously undersubscribed market (see
[Denmark 2.8 GW CfD Tender 2026](/tenders/denmark-2-8gw-cfd-tender-2026.md)), and
would align its design more closely with the
[UK CfD scheme](/policy/uk-contracts-for-difference-scheme.md). The draft is a
consultation text, not law: volumes, the CfD design and the operating period may
change before adoption.
