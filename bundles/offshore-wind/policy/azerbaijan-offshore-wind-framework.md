# Overview

Azerbaijan has no offshore wind farms. Its offshore wind ambitions rest on the
**Caspian Sea**, a landlocked body of water where wind development depends on
national designation of sea areas rather than on any regional or EU-level
framework.

# Designation of a 711.4 km² Water Area (August 2026)

On **12 August 2026**, Azerbaijan's **Cabinet of Ministers** formally designated a
**711.4-square-kilometre water area** in the country's sector of the Caspian Sea
for offshore wind. The designation is a spatial-planning act: it identifies where
offshore wind may be developed, which is the precondition for any subsequent
leasing, permitting or competitive allocation.

Beyond the size of the area and the fact of its designation by the Cabinet of
Ministers, the source item is a short excerpt and supports no further detail — no
coordinates or distance from shore, no water depths or expected foundation type,
no capacity estimate, no named developer, and no tender or award process.

# Why the Caspian Is Distinctive

The Caspian is the bundle's first inland-sea offshore wind market, alongside the
Black Sea framework in [Romania](/policy/romanian-offshore-wind-framework.md) and
the Baltic and North Sea regimes that dominate the rest of this section. Whether
the designated area suits
[fixed-bottom foundations](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md) or
[floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) is not established by the source.
