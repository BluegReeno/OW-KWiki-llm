# Summary

[RWE](/companies/rwe-offshore-wind.md) and the US Department of the Interior reached a settlement agreement, announced in the US on 6 August 2026, under which RWE relinquishes its offshore wind leases in the New York Bight and off the coasts of California and Louisiana in exchange for USD 1.22 billion (around EUR 1.06 billion). The agreement resolves RWE U.S. Offshore's claims against the US government and ends the company's development plans for the three lease areas — a US portfolio of nearly 6 GW comprising the 2.8 GW Community Offshore Wind project off New York and New Jersey, the 1.6 GW Canopy [floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) project off Humboldt County, California, and a 1.24 GW site off Louisiana.

RWE said it had invested more than USD 1 billion in acquiring the leases and developing the projects, but concluded there is "no path forward to permit these projects in the U.S. for the foreseeable future". It had already frozen US offshore wind activities in April 2025, citing political uncertainty, and reports in May 2026 that it was negotiating an exit prompted more than 50 US organisations to urge it to walk away from the talks. The company said it will redeploy capital to projects that can be advanced with greater certainty, including a USD 900 million investment for an indirect 16 per cent stake in the Louisiana LNG project and a USD 300 million turbine reservation agreement for gas peaking projects, and plans roughly EUR 17 billion of US investment over six years to grow generation capacity from about 13 GW today to 22 GW by 2031. RWE stressed it remains focused on growing offshore wind globally, with 18 wind farms operating, four under construction and 6.9 GW secured in the UK's latest auction.

At USD 1.22 billion, this is the largest lease termination settlement announced so far under the [US federal offshore wind leasing framework](/policy/us-boem-leasing-process.md), and the fifth in a series: TotalEnergies (USD 928 million, March 2026, New York Bight and Carolina Long Bay), Bluepoint Wind and Golden State Wind led by [Ocean Winds](/companies/ocean-winds.md) (USD 885 million combined, April 2026), Invenergy (USD 765 million for four leases, June 2026) and Duke Energy's Carolina Long Bay exit (USD 129 million, [June 2026](/digests/2026-06-30-duke-energy-carolina-long-bay-lease-buyout.md)).

Not stated in the article: how the settlement sum compares with the individual lease payments or development expenditure, the payment schedule, whether RWE made any reinvestment commitment tied to the funds (as TotalEnergies, Invenergy and Duke Energy did), the fate of the relinquished lease areas, and whether the deal faces the kind of state-level litigation already filed over the TotalEnergies and Golden State agreements.
