# Italian Startup Testing Offshore Wind Energy Storage Technology Off Calabria

Italian startup **Sizable Energy** has begun testing an
[offshore energy storage](/technology/offshore-energy-storage.md) technology
designed to store electricity from offshore wind farms and other renewables, at
the **Natural Ocean Engineering Laboratory (NOEL)** — the University Mediterranea
of Reggio Calabria's offshore test site off the coast of Calabria, Italy.

The demonstrator, installed in **April 2026**, is a **floating reservoir 50
metres in diameter**. The principle is that of pumped hydro, but instead of an
elevation difference between mountain reservoirs it uses hydrostatic pressure and
gravity offshore: surplus electricity pumps a **dense brine solution** into the
floating reservoir, and when demand rises the brine is released to a **second
reservoir on the seabed**, driving turbines to generate power. The company
positions it as an alternative to battery energy storage systems (BESS). The
campaign at NOEL is planned to validate the technology at pre-industrial scale
and to refine launch and installation methods ahead of commercial deployment.

On **29 July 2026**, France-headquartered logistics provider **GEODIS** said it
had carried out the engineering for the launching, towing and installation of the
storage tank — lifting, transport and installation engineering including crane
positioning, lifting plans, towing procedures and mooring operations, as well as
on-site technical supervision during deployment. The reservoir weighs about **11
tonnes** and its launch required a **350-tonne crane with an outreach of around
50 metres** to meet site and environmental constraints.

No capacity (MW or MWh) figure, round-trip efficiency, water depth, test-campaign
duration, funding or commercial deployment target was reported, so none is
asserted here. No wind farm is connected to the demonstrator.

This is the bundle's first offshore *electricity* storage concept, complementing
[Power-to-X](/technology/power-to-x.md), which addresses the same
generation-versus-demand mismatch by converting the electricity into hydrogen
instead — as demonstrated by
[PosHYdon's first green hydrogen](/digests/2026-07-24-poshydon-first-green-hydrogen.md).
