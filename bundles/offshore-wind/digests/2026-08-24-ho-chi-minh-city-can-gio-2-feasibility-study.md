# Ho Chi Minh City Reviews Feasibility Study for 6 GW Can Gio 2 Offshore Wind Development

offshoreWIND.biz reported on 24 August 2026 that Ho Chi Minh City's Standing
People's Committee has reviewed a feasibility study for
[Can Gio 2](/projects/can-gio-2.md), an offshore wind development proposed at
an installed capacity of **6 GW** and located **10 to 18 kilometres** off the
city's Can Gio district. Vice Chairman of the Ho Chi Minh City People's
Committee **Nguyen Cong Vinh** chaired the meeting on **20 August** at which
the relevant departments heard the feasibility study report for investment in
the project, as recorded in the city government's bulletin for that day.

Can Gio 2 is put forward by a joint venture between the Vietnamese renewable
energy developers [Xuan Cau Holdings](/companies/xuan-cau-holdings.md) and
[Trung Nam](/companies/trung-nam.md), and is being proposed for inclusion in
the project list of Vietnam's revised
[Power Development Plan VIII](/policy/vietnam-power-development-plan-8.md) —
under which, per the Global Wind Energy Council, Vietnam targets 6-17 GW of
offshore wind by 2030-2035 and 113-139 GW by 2050. Vietnamese media put the
project's preliminary investment at **VND 587.73 trillion** (roughly
**EUR 19.2 billion**), financed by equity, commercial loans and export credit
agency funding; the city bulletin itself gives no investment value, schedule
or technical specification.

What stands out is the scale relative to the rest of the Vietnamese thread in
this bundle. Vietnam had appeared here only as a supply-chain and services
market — [PTSC M&C](/companies/ptsc-mc.md) fabricating a substation for
Taiwan's Fengmiao, [Ta San Shang Marine](/companies/ta-san-shang-marine.md)
launching a [CSOV](/digests/2026-08-10-tssm-csov-launch-vietnam.md) at a
Vietnamese yard, and
[EOLOS and Enviros](/digests/2026-08-07-eolos-enviros-vietnam-metocean.md)
partnering on metocean campaigns. This is the bundle's first Vietnamese
*project*, and a single scheme sized at the entire low end of the national
2030-2035 target, sponsored by domestic developers rather than the
international majors — one of which, Equinor, left the market in 2024.

New pages were warranted on the threshold used for
[Sørlige Nordsjø II / Ventyr](/digests/2026-08-24-norway-extends-sorlige-nordsjo-ii-licence-deadline.md):
a named project with a stated capacity, location and sponsor reaching a dated
official milestone, in a market the bundle had no project or policy page for
at all. The two developer pages are deliberately thin — each is named only as
a JV partner, with no other scope stated.

No `tenders` tag: a feasibility study review and a request for inclusion in a
national power development plan is not a call for bids, auction round or
contract award, and no Vietnamese offshore wind tendering mechanism is
described. Nothing beyond the article was asserted: no turbine supplier,
foundation type, water depth or site area, no grid connection or offtake, no
construction or commissioning dates, no further permitting steps, no JV stake
split, no named lenders or export credit agencies, and no relationship to any
"Can Gio 1".
