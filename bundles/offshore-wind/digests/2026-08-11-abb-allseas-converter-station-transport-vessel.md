# Summary

[ABB](/companies/abb.md) has secured a contract with Chinese yard Guangzhou
Shipyard International (GSI) to supply the power equipment for a new offshore
converter station transport vessel being built for
[Allseas](/companies/allseas.md).

What stands out is the cargo the vessel is designed for. Offshore converter
platforms are the heaviest and costliest single structures in an
[HVDC grid connection](/technology/hvdc-grid-connection.md), and the bundle has
followed them almost entirely on the fabrication side —
[Seatrium's UK hub for TenneT's 2 GW programme](/digests/2026-07-30-seatrium-uk-hub-tennet-sofia.md),
[SK Oceanplant's BalWin5 jacket](/digests/2026-07-31-sk-oceanplant-balwin5-jacket.md),
[PTSC M&C's Fengmiao 1 substation load-out](/digests/2026-07-29-ptsc-mc-fengmiao-substation-loadout.md) —
never the tonnage that has to carry them from yard to site. It also lands the
electrical-systems supplier in a shipbuilding item rather than a grid one, and
puts another European owner's offshore wind newbuild in a Chinese yard, in the
same week as [Cadeler's two foundation installation vessels at COSCO](/digests/2026-08-10-cadeler-two-foundation-installation-vessels-cosco.md)
and [ZPMC's second SOV for LD Armateurs](/digests/2026-08-10-zpmc-second-sov-ld-armateurs-nordlicht.md).

Tagged `tenders` because a supply contract award is a first-class tender event
in this bundle; no `tenders/` page was created, since no call for bids or
auction round is involved.

The excerpt is truncated after the opening clause ("to supply the power
[...]"), so nothing further is asserted: no contract value, no description of
the power, propulsion or automation scope, no vessel name, design, dimensions
or lifting/transport capacity, no delivery date, no named projects, converter
platforms or clients the vessel will serve, and no statement on whether it is
the first of a series.
