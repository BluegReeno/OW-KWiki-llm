# Summary

[Vattenfall](/companies/vattenfall.md) has been selected as the winning bidder
for both the [North Sea I Mid (Nordsøen Midt)](/projects/nordsoen-midt.md) and
[Hesselø](/projects/hesselo.md) sites in
[Denmark's latest offshore wind tender](/tenders/denmark-2-8gw-cfd-tender-2026.md).
The two projects have a combined minimum capacity of at least 1.8 GW and are to
be fully operational by 2032 — enough, per the Danish Energy Agency, for the
annual consumption of at least 1.8 million Danish and European households.

Hesselø, in the Kattegat between Denmark and Sweden, has a minimum capacity of
800 MW; North Sea I Mid, about 20 km off Hvide Sande, a minimum of 1 GW. Grid
connection is reserved for both, with a connection point roughly 50 km inland.
Vattenfall bid lowest in both auctions, at a Contract for Difference strike
price of **DKK 504/MWh (~EUR 67.60)** for North Sea I Mid and **DKK 542/MWh
(~EUR 72.70)** for Hesselø, guaranteed for 20 years from commissioning. The
agency received two bids for North Sea I Mid and five for Hesselø; the former
went to Vattenfall Vindkraft A/S on behalf of a company yet to be established,
the latter to Vattenfall Vindkraft Offshore Wind DK P/S. Consent runs 30 years
with a 10-year extension option, and contract signature awaits formal
verification and procurement compliance checks.

These are the first contracts awarded under Denmark's revamped procurement
framework, introduced after the previous unsupported 3 GW tender drew zero bids
in late 2024 and rebuilt around two-sided CfDs with state aid and longer
project timelines. The revised tender, launched in November 2025, covers 2.8 GW
across three projects; the 1 GW Nordsøen Syd (North Sea South) site remains
open, with bids due 20 October 2028.

No turbine supplier, foundation type, turbine count or construction schedule
was reported for either site, and none is asserted here.
