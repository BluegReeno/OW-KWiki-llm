# South Koreans Make Move to Cut Foreign Dependence with Domestic ROV Capability

[Taihan Cable & Solution](/companies/taihan-cable-solution.md) and
[Panstar Robotics](/companies/panstar-robotics.md) — a company established by
the Korea Institute of Robotics & Technology Convergence (KIRO) — signed a
memorandum of understanding on **19 August 2026** to jointly develop
remotely operated vehicles for deployment in South Korea, aiming to cut the
country's reliance on foreign equipment and overseas specialists.

The MoU covers high-performance **ROV trenchers**, their operating
technologies and installation solutions, for use in offshore wind cable
systems and in long-distance grid interconnection and
[HVDC](/technology/hvdc-grid-connection.md) submarine cable projects,
including the **West Coast Energy Highway**. The partners will also carry out
demonstration testing of
[submarine cable burial and protection](/technology/subsea-cable-installation.md)
technologies, pursue commercialisation, take part in national R&D programmes
and jointly explore overseas markets. Chun-won Lee, Executive Vice President
of Taihan, framed the initiative as strengthening the competitiveness of
Korea's submarine cable supply chain and contributing to national energy
security.

It follows a run of Taihan supply-chain moves: Submarine Cable Plant 2 under
construction for 640 kV HVDC cables, a completed 640 kV cable test centre,
and the addition of the CLV **Skandi Connector** alongside **PALOS**, Korea's
first offshore wind-dedicated cable-laying vessel.

# Why It Matters

Trenching has been the part of the cable chain Korea did not own. Taihan can
manufacture the cable and, since 2026, lay it from its own vessels; burial
still depends on imported ROV spreads and the crews that run them. Developing
the trencher domestically closes that gap and puts a cable maker — not a
marine contractor — in control of the whole spread, the same vertical
integration [NKT](/companies/nkt.md) is pursuing in Europe with the T-3600
and the NKT Eleonora.
