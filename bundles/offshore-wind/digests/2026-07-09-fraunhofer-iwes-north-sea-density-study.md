# Fraunhofer IWES Study on German North Sea Density and Cross-Border Expansion

A new study by the **Fraunhofer Institute for Wind Energy Systems (Fraunhofer IWES)**,
initiated by the German Association of Energy and Water Industries (BDEW) and the
German Offshore Wind Energy Association (BWO), finds that a **less dense development**
of offshore wind in the German Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) of the North Sea — plus
relocating some capacity to neighbouring countries — could both increase yield and
reduce costs.

Key points:

- Under Germany's current 70 GW by 2045 target and planned land use, offshore wind
  utilisation would fall far short of technical potential. Densely packed wind farms
  create shading/wake effects that reduce each other's output, cutting into the
  ~5,000 achievable full-load hours.
- Scenarios envision installing up to **20 GW in neighbouring countries' waters** and
  feeding that electricity into the German grid, with higher capacity utilisation and
  lower overall construction and operating costs.
- Depending on scenario, full-load hours and yields could rise by **6–13 %** on average,
  while cost per MWh could fall by **6–11 %**.
- With a stronger focus on area yield and cost efficiency, ~50–60 GW could be installed
  in the German EEZ by 2045; together with up to 20 GW from neighbours, the 70 GW target
  would remain realistic.

The study reinforces the case for Europe-wide coordination of capacity and area
development plans — a theme of the [EU Offshore Renewable Energy Strategy](/policy/eu-offshore-wind-strategy.md).
