# Summary

offshoreWIND.biz reported on **28 August 2026** that [BW Ideol](/companies/bw-ideol.md)'s **2 MW Floatgen** floating wind turbine has been in operation for **eight years** and has produced **40 GWh** of electricity.

A [Floatgen](/projects/floatgen.md) project page was created: the bundle had no entry for it despite carrying the company page and several other single-turbine floating demonstrators. A cumulative generation figure over an eight-year run is a durable operating fact, the same threshold that earned [Rudong](/projects/rudong.md) a page for passing 10 TWh. The [BW Ideol](/companies/bw-ideol.md) page gained an operating track record section, and [Floating Offshore Wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) a cross-reference.

# Notes

What stands out is the contrast within the same technology thread. Every other floating item recorded here recently has been about getting hardware into the water or qualified — [Encomara's Squid](/companies/encomara.md) reaching commercialisation the same day, [MacGregor](/companies/macgregor.md)'s floating-to-floating gangway certification, BW Ideol's own [NGE fabrication partnership](/companies/bw-ideol.md) in July 2026 — while this is the opposite end of the curve: an eight-year, uninterrupted service record, which is what a sector still arguing about floater durability actually lacks. It also lands three weeks after the [TetraSpar Demonstrator](/projects/tetraspar-demonstrator.md) started decommissioning, so within one month the bundle records both an early floater retiring and another passing eight years still generating.

No `tenders` tag: a generation milestone is not a call for bids, auction round or contract award.

Nothing beyond the excerpt was asserted: no site location, water depth or distance from shore, no floater type or mooring configuration, no turbine supplier or model, no commissioning date beyond "eight years", no annual output, availability or capacity factor, no owner, operator, funding source or offtaker, and no plan for the unit's remaining life.
