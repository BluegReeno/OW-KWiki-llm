# Monopile Lowering as a Critical Jack-Up Load Case

Branded content published by jack-up and vessel designer GustoMSC on
offshoreWIND.biz. Its argument: as offshore wind monopiles continue to grow
in size and weight — a direct consequence of
[turbine scaling](/technology/turbine-scaling.md) and deeper, harsher sites —
the installation phase itself is becoming a design driver, with the lowering
of the monopile through the splash zone emerging as a critical load case for
the jack-up vessels performing the work.

This is a technical opinion/marketing piece rather than a news event: no
contract, project or vessel commitment is announced. It is recorded here as
context on the engineering pressures behind
[fixed-bottom foundations](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md) and the
installation fleet serving them (see, for instance,
[Cadeler](/companies/cadeler.md)'s fleet expansion and
[EEW Group](/companies/eew-group.md)'s monopile manufacturing capacity).

The item supplied to this bundle was a truncated excerpt, so no specific
load figures, monopile dimensions, vessel classes or design methods are
reproduced here.
