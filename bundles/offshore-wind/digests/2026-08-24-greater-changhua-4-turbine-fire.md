# Summary

[Ørsted](/companies/orsted.md) said a fire was reported on a wind turbine at its
**Greater Changhua 4** offshore wind farm off Changhua County, Taiwan, and that
the turbine was taken offline. The item was published by offshoreWIND.biz on
24 August 2026 and lists the [Greater Changhua](/projects/changhua.md) 2b & 4
project, Ørsted and [Siemens Gamesa](/companies/siemens-gamesa.md) as its
subjects.

Greater Changhua 4 is the 583 MW phase of the 920 MW GC2b & 4 project, which
uses 66 Siemens Gamesa SG 14-236 turbines — the first deployment of that model
anywhere in the world. Ørsted's H1 2026 results, published four days earlier,
had put GC2b & 4 at [approximately 85 per cent
complete](/digests/2026-08-20-orsted-h1-2026-construction-update.md), with all
42 GC4 turbines commissioned, grid-connected and generating, and full
commissioning targeted for Q3 2026.

# Why it matters

An in-service turbine fire on a fleet-leading turbine model is a durable
operating fact, so it was folded into the existing
[Changhua](/projects/changhua.md) project page rather than left as news noise —
it is the first turbine-level incident recorded in this bundle, against a page
otherwise built entirely from installation and commissioning milestones. It also
lands on the SG 14-236's first commercial fleet, days after the same project was
reported on course for Q3 2026 commissioning.

# What the item does not say

The excerpt supports only the fire, the turbine being taken offline and Ørsted's
confirmation. It states no cause, no damage assessment, no injuries or personnel
detail, no date for the incident itself, no return-to-service estimate, and no
revised commissioning date or output impact for GC2b & 4. Nothing was added to
the [Siemens Gamesa](/companies/siemens-gamesa.md) page: no fault, design issue
or fleet action is attributed to the manufacturer in the item, only its listing
as a category. No `tenders` tag: an operational incident is not a call for bids,
auction round or contract award.
