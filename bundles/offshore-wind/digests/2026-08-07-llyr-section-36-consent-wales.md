# Summary

The Welsh government has granted **Section 36 consent** to the
[Llŷr Wind Farm](/projects/llyr.md), the [floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md)
test and demonstration programme developed by [Cierco Energy](/companies/cierco-energy.md)
through Floventis Energy, its joint venture with SBM Offshore, off the Pembrokeshire coast
in the Celtic Sea.

Section 36 of the Electricity Act 1989 is the consent required to construct and operate a
generating station; for projects in Welsh waters the decision sits with the Welsh
Ministers. The grant comes three days after the
[Agreement for Lease signed with The Crown Estate](/digests/2026-08-04-llyr-agreement-for-lease-crown-estate.md)
on 4 August 2026, so the programme secured seabed rights and development consent within
the same week — the two foundational permissions before a project can move toward
construction.

The bundle already records the programme as two 100 MW sites, Llŷr 1 and Llŷr 2, each with
six to eight turbines, about 40 km offshore at depths averaging 60–70 metres, intended to
compare two different floating technologies. The article headline and excerpt refer to
"the Llŷr Wind Farm" in the singular; whether the consent covers one site or both is not
stated and is not asserted here.

Not a tender item — a consent decision, not a call for bids or an award — so nothing new is
tagged `tenders`, though the project and company pages keep the tag from the Crown Estate
leasing outcome.

The excerpt is truncated after the opening clause, so nothing further was asserted: no
consented capacity, turbine count or tip height, no consent conditions or environmental
mitigations, no date of application or examination history, no marine licence status, no
turbine supplier or floater selection, no construction schedule, grid connection point or
route to market.
