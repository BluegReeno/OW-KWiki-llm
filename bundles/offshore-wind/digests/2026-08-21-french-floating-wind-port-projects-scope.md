# Summary

Follow-up detail on the five port infrastructure projects selected by the French
government on 30 July 2026 to share **EUR 258.5 million** of
[France 2030 funding administered by ADEME](/policy/france-2030-floating-wind-port-funding.md),
awarded after a call for projects launched in 2024. The programme prepares French
ports for the construction of [floating wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) farms
against a national target of nearly 6 GW by 2040.

The allocations: **Marseille-Fos EUR 82.3 million**, **Nantes-Saint-Nazaire
nearly EUR 60 million**, **Brest EUR 58 million**, **Port-la-Nouvelle EUR 48
million** and **Cherbourg EUR 10.5 million**. The government said the money goes
primarily into constructing or modifying quays, storage areas and access
infrastructure able to take the large components floating wind requires — for
foundation manufacture and assembly, and for integrating turbines onto the
floaters.

Scope by port: Marseille-Fos's **DEOS** covers 30 hectares of port land, 400 m of
quay dredged to 17 m and a 31-hectare floating storage area, for a total
investment of EUR 303 million, with an infrastructure design-and-build tender
planned for autumn 2026, construction from early 2028 and phased deliveries from
late 2029 to mid-2030; Nantes-Saint-Nazaire's **Éole** centres on a 530 m
turbine-integration quay sized for 500-600 MW of throughput a year, in a project
costing over EUR 200 million; Brest's **InFloW** reinforces quays and adds
logistics and industrial capacity at its marine renewable energy terminal;
Port-la-Nouvelle's **NOUVEOL** aims at a fully integrated assembly and
heavy-maintenance platform for French Mediterranean floating wind by 2030; and
Cherbourg builds **Flamands 0**, a 140-160 m heavy-duty quay rated for 15-50
tonnes per square metre.
