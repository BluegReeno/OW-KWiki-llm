# Summary

On 24 July 2026 the Swedish government completed decisions on all offshore wind
permit applications under review in the country's exclusive economic zone,
**approving two projects and rejecting eleven**.

Approved:

- **[Fyrskeppet Offshore](/projects/fyrskeppet-offshore.md)**
  ([Skyborn Renewables](/companies/skyborn.md)) — ~54 km east of Söderhamn in the
  southern Gulf of Bothnia; permit for up to 93 turbines, expected 8-11 TWh/year.
  Earlier project information described 187 turbines totalling around 2.8 GW.
- **[Vidar](/projects/vidar.md)** ([Vattenfall](/companies/vattenfall.md) and
  Zephyr) — ~35 km southwest of Strömstad in the northern Skagerrak; 2 GW, up to 75
  turbines, up to 7.8 TWh/year.

Both must be completed within ten years, by 2036, and both still need separate
permits for the subsea export cables to the onshore grid before construction.

Rejected: Bothnia Offshore Lambda North, Bothnia Offshore Sigma, Eystrasalt
Offshore, Gretas Klackar 1, Gävle Öst Havsvindpark, Mareld, Najaderna, Olof
Skötkonung, Polargrund Offshore, Sylen, and Västvind. The government held that the
Swedish Armed Forces had found these wind farms would conflict with areas of
national interest for military defence, and that mitigation measures and the
projects' fossil-free generation did not outweigh that. The related Continental
Shelf Act cable permits were dismissed without examination. The refusals are not
permanent exclusions: the areas could be reconsidered once Sweden moves to an
**auction-based system** for offshore wind.

This repeats the pattern of November 2024, when the government approved Vattenfall's
Poseidon and rejected 13 applications on the same grounds, after earlier approvals
of Kattegatt Syd and Galene.

Immediately after the approval, Vattenfall CEO Anna Borg was cited by Reuters
saying the company **will not proceed with Vidar at this time** as current market
conditions do not make it commercially viable — a permitted-but-paused project.
