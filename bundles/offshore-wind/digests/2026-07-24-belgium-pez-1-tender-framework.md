# Belgium Approves New Tender Framework for First Princess Elisabeth Zone Offshore Wind Site

The Belgian federal government has approved a new regulatory framework for relaunching the
tender for the first offshore wind site in the Princess Elisabeth Zone
([PEZ 1](/tenders/belgium-princess-elisabeth-zone-pez-1.md), ~700 MW), one year after the
original tender was cancelled in July 2025 over legal and regulatory uncertainties around the
support mechanism, state aid approval, the energy island's delivery schedule and onshore grid
reinforcements.

The revised rules introduce a single two-sided CfD in which the lowest strike price bid wins,
remove the strike price cap, extend the maximum construction period from four to five years,
and make citizen participation voluntary. The tender is also aligned with the EU's Net-Zero
Industry Act, with binding prequalification criteria on cybersecurity, sustainability,
responsible business conduct and supply chain resilience — including a rule that at least 75%
of the project's turbines cannot be manufactured or assembled in China, plus limits on
Chinese-made critical components and permanent magnets.

A separate royal decree sets 1 October 2031 as the latest in-service date for the first phase
of Elia's Princess Elisabeth energy island (MOG II), the world's first artificial energy
island, 45 km off the Belgian coast. Grid infrastructure and the tender will now run in
parallel rather than sequentially. The revised rules still go to the Belgian Council of State
and must be notified to the European Commission under state aid rules before the auction is
relaunched.

The zone comprises three sites totalling up to 3.5 GW, with the two later sites planned at
1,400 MW each.
