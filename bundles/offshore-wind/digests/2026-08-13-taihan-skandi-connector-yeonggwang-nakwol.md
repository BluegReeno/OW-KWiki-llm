# Summary

South Korea's [Taihan Cable & Solution](/companies/taihan-cable-solution.md)
held a safe operation ceremony on **12 August 2026** at Orient Shipyard in
Gamcheon Port, Busan, for the cable-laying vessel (CLV) *Skandi Connector*,
ahead of full-scale deployment on offshore wind work.

Taihan announced in May 2026 that it had bought the 2016-built vessel from
Norway's DOF Group — reported as the second offshore wind-dedicated CLV
introduced in Korea — and [took delivery in early July](/digests/2026-07-17-taihan-second-cable-laying-vessel.md).
Designed by Dutch shipbuilder Damen, the Skandi Connector is a
10,000-tonne-class, 138.4-metre vessel.

Its **first offshore wind assignment** is the Yeonggwang Nakwol offshore
wind project in South Korea, where it is scheduled to deploy in August 2026
to carry out burial operations for submarine cables. A Taihan official
framed the vessel as strengthening the company's installation
competitiveness and its turnkey, end-to-end submarine cable offering.

The move parallels compatriot [LS Cable & System](/companies/ls-cable-system.md)'s
[ultra-large CLV under construction in Türkiye](/digests/2026-07-06-ls-cable-clv-tersan-shipyard.md),
and lands in a summer of cable-installation tonnage being added across Asia —
[ZTT's newly launched CLV](/digests/2026-08-07-ztt-lan-hai-ling-hang-cable-laying-vessel.md)
and [ZPMC's multipurpose CLV for Far East Cable](/digests/2026-07-23-zpmc-clv-far-east-cable.md).
Taihan's route is different: buying existing European tonnage rather than
ordering a newbuild, which is what puts it on a wind farm this month.

No project page was created for Yeonggwang Nakwol: the article supports only
its name and that cable burial starts there in August 2026 — no capacity,
