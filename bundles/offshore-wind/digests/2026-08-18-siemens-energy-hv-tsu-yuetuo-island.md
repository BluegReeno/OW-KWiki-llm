# Summary

[Siemens Energy](/companies/siemens-energy.md) has recorded the first
deployment at an offshore wind project of its lightweight integrated offshore
substation: the **High-Voltage TranSwitch Unit (HV-TSU)** has been installed at
the [Yuetuo Island Offshore Wind Farm](/projects/yuetuo-island.md) in China.
The design integrates key high-voltage components into a more compact package,
which the company says significantly reduces topside weight while keeping the
reliability offshore operation requires — an approach it presents as a way to
optimise project execution, reduce complexity and speed up grid connection for
the next generation of offshore wind.

[ZPMC](/companies/zpmc.md) built the substation's topside module, jacket
foundation and foundation pin piles. The 300 MW topside measures 45 m long,
38 m wide and 23 m high, weighing approximately 1,700 tonnes. The wind farm —
Guodian Tangshan Leting Yuetuo Island Offshore Wind Farm (Phase I), in North
China — will have a total installed capacity of 304 MW.

# Why It Matters

The bundle's [offshore substation](/technology/offshore-substations.md) thread
has so far been about prefabricated, factory-tested modules — Siemens Energy's
Offshore Transformer Module at [Inch Cape](/projects/inch-cape.md), the
[PTSC M&C-built topside for Fengmiao 1](/digests/2026-07-29-ptsc-mc-fengmiao-substation-loadout.md)
— and this pushes the same logic one step further, integrating the high-voltage
equipment itself to cut the weight that has to be lifted offshore. It is also
the first time a European grid supplier's new substation concept appears here
debuting in China rather than in Europe, on a topside from a yard the bundle
knew until now only for [vessels](/digests/2026-08-10-zpmc-second-sov-ld-armateurs-nordlicht.md).

No `tenders` tag: this is a hardware installation, not a call for bids, auction
round or contract award, and no contract value or client procurement process is
stated. The reporting covers the substation only — no turbine supplier, model
or count for Yuetuo Island, no array foundation or installation contractors, no
installation vessel for the topside lift, no weight comparison against a
conventional substation of the same rating, and no first-power or commissioning
date.
