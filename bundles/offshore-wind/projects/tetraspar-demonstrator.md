# Overview

The **TetraSpar Demonstrator** is a single-turbine [floating offshore wind](/technology/floating-wind.md) prototype deployed at the **Marine Energy Test Centre (METCentre)** off Karmøy, western Norway. It was built to prove out **Stiesdal Offshore**'s TetraSpar concept: a floater assembled from standardised tubular members and joints — a tetrahedral space frame with a suspended keel — designed for industrial-scale serial fabrication and quayside assembly rather than one-off heavy fabrication.

The unit carries a [Siemens Gamesa](/companies/siemens-gamesa.md) turbine of roughly 3.6 MW and has been in the water since 2021, operating as a test and data-gathering platform. Its ownership consortium brought together Stiesdal Offshore with [RWE](/companies/rwe-offshore-wind.md), Shell and TEPCO Renewable Power.

# Status

On **3 August 2026**, offshoreWIND.biz reported that **decommissioning work has started** on the demonstrator at the test site. The available excerpt stops there: no contractor, vessel spread, disassembly method, timetable, tow destination, reuse or recycling plan for the floater and keel, and no reason given for ending the campaign at this point. None of that is asserted here.

The demonstrator's retirement is notable in its own right — floating wind is still young enough that few prototypes have reached end of life, so the removal and disposal of a floater and its mooring system is itself a first-of-a-kind exercise, and the counterpart to the deployment milestones that dominate the sector's news flow.
