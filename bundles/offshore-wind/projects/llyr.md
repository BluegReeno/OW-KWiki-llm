# Overview

**Llŷr 1** and **Llŷr 2** are two test and demonstration [floating offshore wind](/technology/floating-wind.md)
projects off the coast of Wales, each with a capacity of **100 MW** and each comprising
**six to eight turbines**. The sites lie off the Pembrokeshire coast in the Celtic Sea,
approximately **40 km offshore** at water depths averaging **60–70 metres**.

The two projects are expected to use **two different types of floating wind technology**,
making them a comparative testbed for foundations, mooring systems and dynamic cabling
ahead of commercial-scale Celtic Sea deployment.

# Ownership and Development

The Llŷr sites were selected for development in **2021** in The Crown Estate's leasing
opportunity for early commercial-scale floating wind in the Celtic Sea.
[Cierco Energy](/companies/cierco-energy.md) secured the two sites in a joint venture
with **SBM Offshore** called **Floventis Energy**. Public consultation for Llŷr 1 opened
in early 2024.

In July 2025, Cierco Energy signed a Memorandum of Understanding with
**Marine Power Systems (MPS)** on potentially deploying the **PelaFlex** floating
technology at one of the Llŷr demonstration sites.

# Status

On **4 August 2026**, Cierco Energy signed an **Agreement for Lease (AfL)** with
The Crown Estate for both projects. Scott Harper, Managing Director at Cierco Energy,
described the demonstration projects as "vital stepping stones, giving us the chance to
test world-leading offshore foundations, mooring systems and dynamic cabling before
scaling up to commercial deployments", and said successful delivery would catalyse an
industrial ecosystem spanning offshore fabrication, engineering, advanced manufacturing
and technology exports.

On **7 August 2026**, three days later, the **Welsh government granted Section 36 consent**
to the Llŷr Wind Farm — the consent required under the Electricity Act 1989 to construct
and operate a generating station in Welsh waters, a decision devolved to the Welsh
Ministers. The source refers to the consent in the singular ("the Llŷr Wind Farm"); which
of the two sites it covers, and whether it covers both, is not stated here.

Together with the Agreement for Lease, this gives the demonstration programme its two
foundational permissions — seabed rights and development consent — within the same week.

No turbine supplier, final floater selection per site, construction schedule, grid
connection point or route to market has been reported.
