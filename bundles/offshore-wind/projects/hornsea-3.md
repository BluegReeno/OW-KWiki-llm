# Overview

Hornsea 3 is a 2.9 GW offshore wind farm under construction around
120 kilometres off the coast of Norfolk in the UK North Sea. It is owned by
[Ørsted](/companies/orsted.md) together with Apollo-managed funds, joined in
May 2026 by Mubadala Investment Company. When operational — expected in 2027 —
it is set to become the world's largest single offshore wind farm, succeeding
[Hornsea 2](/projects/hornsea-2.md), and is described as able to power more
than 3.3 million UK homes.

The wind farm will comprise 197 [Siemens Gamesa](/companies/siemens-gamesa.md)
SG 14-236 DD turbines rated at 14 MW excluding power boost, on
[fixed-bottom](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md) XXL monopiles, with
[HVDC](/technology/hvdc-grid-connection.md) export to shore.

# Iceni battery energy storage system

Hornsea 3 has a co-located onshore battery energy storage system named **Iceni**,
rated **300 MW / 600 MWh**. It is one of the UK's first large-scale co-location
projects under the **Offshore Transmission Network Review (OTNR)**: instead of
securing a grid connection of its own, Iceni shares Hornsea 3's transmission
infrastructure and onshore grid connection. [Arenko](/companies/arenko.md), whose
Nimbus platform was selected to operate it, describes it as the world's first
utility-scale battery directly integrated into offshore transmission
infrastructure — see [offshore energy storage](/technology/offshore-energy-storage.md).

- **June 2024** — [Ørsted](/companies/orsted.md) takes FID, specifying a Tesla
  system of 600 MWh and 300 MW.
- **March 2025** — groundbreaking near Norwich, on the same site as the
  Hornsea 3 onshore converter station.
- **18 August 2026** — Ørsted selects Arenko's **Nimbus** optimisation, asset
  management and trading platform to forecast market and asset conditions,
  allocate battery capacity in real time, automate trading across power markets,
  and handle the technical, data and controls integration with the wind farm.
- **Q1 2027** — operations scheduled to begin.

# Construction timeline

- **February 2026** — first Hornsea 3 monopiles arrive in the UK from Spain
  (Haizea).
- **March 2026** — first offshore substation completed.
- **May 2026** — first monopile installed offshore; Cadeler's *Wind Ally*
  starts foundation installation at site.
- **6 August 2026** — Haizea delivers what it describes as the largest
  monopiles ever manufactured in Southern Europe: over 105 m long, 11 m in
  diameter, more than 2,300 tonnes each, loaded out at the Port of Bilbao.
- **20 August 2026** — Ørsted's first-half 2026 results put Hornsea 3 at
  approximately **30 per cent complete**, with foundation and export cable
  installation progressing offshore and continuing work with the grid operator
  on the reinforcements required for the onshore grid connection. Commissioning
  is on track for **Q4 2027 or Q1 2028**.
