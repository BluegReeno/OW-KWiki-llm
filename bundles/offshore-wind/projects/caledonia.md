# Overview

**Caledonia** is a 2 GW offshore wind farm proposed in the Moray Firth, around 40 km off the coasts of Wick and Banff in Scotland, developed by [Ocean Winds](/companies/ocean-winds.md) — the 50-50 joint venture of EDP Renewables and ENGIE. The project comprises two wind farms, **North and South Caledonia**, covering 429 square kilometres with up to 140 wind turbines.

Water depths range between 40 and 100 metres, with roughly three-quarters of the site shallow enough for [fixed-bottom foundations](/technology/fixed-bottom-foundations.md) — jacket foundations of the type Ocean Winds optimised at Moray East. Caledonia North is planned entirely on fixed-bottom foundations, while Caledonia South is to use a mix of fixed-bottom and [floating foundations](/technology/floating-wind.md).

# Permitting and Schedule

Ocean Winds submitted consent applications to Aberdeenshire Council and the Scottish government's Marine Directorate in late 2024. Aberdeenshire Council granted full onshore consent earlier in 2026, with onshore works planned to start in 2028. On 17 July 2026 the Scottish government granted **offshore consent**, which Ocean Winds said clears the final major regulatory hurdle, allowing the project to advance to the next phase of delivery.

Offshore construction is expected to begin in 2030, subject to the project securing a Contract for Difference (CfD) and reaching final investment decision (FID).
